# Literary sources — ScholarDesk 1.2

These 12 short passages were located online and checked against the linked editions or archival reproductions on 21 November 2026. They are human-authored historical writing, not newly generated jokes or invented quotations. There is no claim that the application, its interface labels, or this explanatory documentation was written by these authors.

The words in quotation marks are copied from the sources. Outer quotation marks, line wrapping and straight rather than curly apostrophes are display typography. Short fragments are labelled as excerpts; they are not presented as complete sentences from the original. Original dates below refer to the works, not the online editions.

## Context matters

- Austen's reading line is ironic: Caroline Bingley is trying to attract Darcy's attention, then soon sets her book aside. It is credited as character dialogue, not a statement of Austen's personal beliefs.
- Wilde's diary and smart lines are spoken by Gwendolen. The latter plays on smart as fashionable as well as clever. Neither is a statement about any student's marks.
- Carroll's lines belong to a nonsense fantasy. The Cheshire Cat's line is literary humour and is not a description of students or of mental health.
- Tennyson's Ulysses speaks in a dramatic monologue. The quotation is not used to define academic standing.
- Franklin's credit records the proverb's publication in his almanac; it does not assert that he invented the saying.

## Where to discover them

- Overview: Dickinson's opening fragment.
- Directory, profile prompt and no-result searches: Alice's question about identity.
- Add and edit forms: the King's instruction and Alice's exclamation, with Franklin's short proverb below the fields.
- Academic insights: the opening of Tennyson's closing line, marked as a title excerpt.
- Export confirmation: Gwendolen's diary joke, after the saved-file location.
- Sidebar: a complete short passage with author credit. **Another passage** cycles through all 12 without a timer or animation.
- Reading shelf: every passage, source, location and context; **Copy with credit** retains the author, work and link; **Read original** opens only the chosen fixed source URL.
- Console: contextual screen quotations and menu option 9 for the complete shelf and source details.

No quotation is inserted into student data files or CSV records. The app works offline; a network connection is only needed if a user chooses to open an original source. No student information is included in those links.

## Reuse choices

The selection is restricted to brief excerpts from historical works, principally public-domain originals. The Dickinson source is a modern edited reading edition; only its four-word opening fragment is reproduced. No modern web author's explanatory prose, contemporary joke collection, site design, or full poem is copied into the application. Context notes and interface implementation are explanatory application material, separate from the credited literary wording.
